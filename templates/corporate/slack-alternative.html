Common questions

How is Zulip different from Slack threads?

Slack threads hang off a single message, and you have to decide to open one. In Zulip, every message belongs to a named topic within its channel, so every discussion is a separate conversation by default. The channel reads as a list of conversations instead of one interleaved stream. Learn more about why topics work.

What does Zulip cost, and is there a free plan?

Zulip Cloud has a free plan that shows your 10,000 most recent messages; older messages are preserved, and reappear if you upgrade. Self-hosting Zulip is free for any number of users. Nonprofits, education, and open-source projects qualify for free or sponsored plans.

Can I self-host Zulip, and what does it take?

Yes. Zulip Cloud works just like Slack does: sign up and go. Organizations with special requirements can also self-host the same software. Zulip installs on a single server with a documented installer and a supported upgrade path.

Can I import my Slack workspace?

Yes. Import your existing organization from Slack, including message history, users, channels, and custom emoji.

Are there mobile and desktop apps?

Zulip has apps for iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, and Linux, plus the web app, with fine-grained control over what you're notified about.

Which integrations does Zulip have?

130+ native integrations, a Slack-compatible incoming webhook, Zapier support, and a full API for building your own.

Where does Zulip fall short of Slack?

Calls run through an integrated provider (Jitsi Meet by default, or Zoom, Webex, and others) rather than inside Zulip itself, its app directory is smaller than Slack's, and fewer people arrive already knowing the product. If those trade-offs matter more to your team than organized conversations, Slack may be the better fit.

Who's behind Zulip?

Zulip is stewarded by the nonprofit Zulip Foundation. The paid team provides leadership, expertise and support for the most active open-source development community of any team chat software.
